# 10 Essential Financial Ratios for Value Investors

Financial ratios are crucial tools for value investors to evaluate companies and make informed investment decisions. Here are the most important ratios you should understand and use in your analysis.

## 1. Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio

### What It Tells You
- How much investors are willing to pay for each dollar of earnings
- Whether a stock is potentially overvalued or undervalued
- Market expectations for future growth

### How to Use It
- Compare with industry averages
- Look at historical P/E trends
- Consider along with growth rates (PEG ratio)

Typical value range: 10-20x for mature companies

## 2. Price-to-Book (P/B) Ratio

### What It Tells You
- The market value relative to accounting value
- Whether you're paying a premium for assets
- Potential margin of safety

### How to Use It
- Compare with return on equity (ROE)
- Consider industry standards
- Account for asset quality

Typical value range: <1.5x for value stocks

## 3. Debt-to-Equity Ratio

### What It Tells You
- Financial leverage
- Balance sheet strength
- Risk level

### How to Use It
- Compare with industry averages
- Consider business cycle position
- Evaluate interest coverage

Ideal range: <2.0 for most industries

## 4. Return on Equity (ROE)

### What It Tells You
- Management's efficiency
- Profitability relative to shareholder investment
- Competitive advantage strength

### How to Use It
- Compare with cost of equity
- Look for consistent trends
- Consider along with leverage

Good range: >15% consistently

## 5. Free Cash Flow Yield

### What It Tells You
- Cash generating efficiency
- Dividend sustainability
- Investment capacity

### How to Use It
- Compare with bond yields
- Look for stable or growing trends
- Consider capital requirements

Attractive range: >5% in current market

## 6. Operating Margin

### What It Tells You
- Operational efficiency
- Pricing power
- Cost control

### How to Use It
- Track trends over time
- Compare with competitors
- Consider industry dynamics

## 7. Current Ratio

### What It Tells You
- Short-term solvency
- Working capital management
- Liquidity position

### How to Use It
- Compare with quick ratio
- Consider industry norms
- Look at trends

Safe range: >1.5

## 8. Payout Ratio

### What It Tells You
- Dividend sustainability
- Earnings retention
- Growth potential

### How to Use It
- Compare with industry averages
- Consider company maturity
- Look at historical trends

Sustainable range: 30-60%

## 9. Enterprise Value/EBITDA

### What It Tells You
- Total business value relative to earnings
- Acquisition value
- Capital structure neutral comparison

### How to Use It
- Compare across different capital structures
- Consider industry standards
- Look for trends

Value range: <10x for most industries

## 10. Interest Coverage Ratio

### What It Tells You
- Debt service capability
- Financial risk
- Earnings cushion

### How to Use It
- Compare with debt levels
- Consider interest rate environment
- Look at stability

Safe range: >3x

## Putting It All Together

No single ratio tells the complete story. Consider these guidelines:

1. Use ratios in combination
2. Compare with industry peers
3. Look for trends over time
4. Consider business cycle position
5. Account for company-specific factors

Remember: These ratios are tools for analysis, not buy/sell signals. Always consider the broader context and use them as part of a comprehensive investment strategy.
